ABOUT // STEWARDSHIP

A standard built in the open, by the people it has to serve.

DTPR did not start as a specification. It started as a question asked in public workshops: what would you need to know about the technology around you before you could trust it?

01 // HOW IT STARTEDDraft copy

Public space filled up with technology faster than anyone built a way to talk about it. Sensors, cameras, counters and models arrived with procurement notices and terms of service, but nothing a person walking past could actually read.

DTPR began by asking communities what was missing. Those sessions produced a short, stubborn list of questions — who is accountable, what is it for, what is collected, and what happens next. The standard is the answer to that list, not to a technical committee's agenda.

  1. 2020

    Co-design sessions produce the first question set, and an ask to imagine the standard five years out.

  2. 2022

    Early municipal pilots put signage and taxonomy in front of residents in real public space.

  3. 2023

    Finalist for the World Smart City Innovation Award in Barcelona; deployments reach three continents.

  4. 2025

    NSF POSE Phase I award to build an open-source ecosystem around the standard.

02 // WHAT WE HOLD TOPRINCIPLES

Three commitments the standard is built on.

LEGIBILITY

Written to be read

If a person walking past cannot understand it, it has not been disclosed. Plain language and icons come before specification detail.

RECIPROCITY

Accountability goes both ways

Disclosure without a channel for response is a notice, not trust. Every deployment can carry a way to answer back.

OPENNESS

Adoptable without permission

Published under CC BY 4.0, developed in public, and extended by the communities and cities that use it.

03 // STEWARDSHIP

Held in trust, not owned.

DTPR is an open standard published under CC BY 4.0. Anyone can use it, adapt it, or build on it without permission or fee.

Day-to-day stewardship — taxonomy governance, releases, and the shared asset library — is carried by Helpful Places, with design and research support from Superbloom. Work on the open-source ecosystem is supported by an NSF POSE Phase I award.
