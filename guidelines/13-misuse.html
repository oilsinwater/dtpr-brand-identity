13 · Misuse & Don'ts

What the brand is not.

As important as the correct examples. Showing what DTPR looks like when its rules are violated makes the rules legible — and gives anyone reviewing work a clear basis for "this doesn't ship." Misuse examples are part of the brand, not an aside.

RULES REVIEW CHECKLIST RECIPE B
Rule of thumb. If the work you're reviewing makes any of the mistakes below, send it back. A clear "no, this is wrong" is faster than three rounds of "could you adjust this slightly."

Mark & lockup

D-01 · STRETCH

Don't stretch the mark.

The hexagon's six sides are equal. Stretching it horizontally or vertically distorts the geometry the mark is built on.

D-02 · ROTATE

Don't rotate the mark.

The mark sits in the same orientation as the wordmark. Rotation breaks the lockup balance.

D-03 · FILL CHANGES

Don't recolor the mark in arbitrary hues.

Chartreuse, salmon, coral, and other accent colors are reserved for status/live-data signals only. The mark stays brand-navy, white, or monochrome.

D-04 · LOW CONTRAST

Don't fade the mark to invisibility.

The watermark variant is the only acceptable low-contrast state, and it's already in the system. Don't reduce opacity further on the mark itself.

Color

D-05 · CHARTREUSE TEXT

Don't use chartreuse for text or borders.

Chartreuse is reserved for the live-data beacon and primary CTA. Body text, headings, and borders stay navy or warm-paper.

This is wrong
D-06 · CHARTREUSE BORDER

Don't frame cards or sections in chartreuse.

Chartreuse frame implies "live / interactive." Static content framed in chartreuse reads as "this is the action" when it isn't.

Looks live but isn't
D-07 · CHARTREUSE-ON-CHARTREUSE

Don't put chartreuse text on a chartreuse background.

Both elements lose the. Contrast failure.

UNREADABLE
D-08 · ARBITRARY ACCENTS

Don't introduce new accent colors.

Rose, lime, mint, gold, teal — all forbidden outside the canonical palette. If a design needs more than navy + blue + chartreuse + paper, return to the values (01) and reduce.

Off-brand

Typography

D-09 · MONO UI

Don't set Source Code Pro as body copy.

Source Code Pro is for code, schema keys, taxonomy codes, and live-data indicators. UI text is Public Sans.

Don't do this for body copy
D-10 · ITALIC EVERYWHERE

Don't italicize more than one sentence per page.

Source Serif 4 italic is the editorial moment — once per surface, in a hero statement. Multiple italic blocks drain the gesture of its weight.

Italic Italic again Italic again
D-11 · BLURRED TEXT

Don't ship text behind a blur.

Frosted glass over legible text reads as "loading" or "disabled." Either commit to the frosted surface or move the text out from under it.

DTPR
D-12 · ALL CAPS BODY

Don't set body copy in all caps.

Mono caps are fine for taxonomy codes, schema keys, and section badges. Body paragraphs in caps reads as shouting and breaks the calm register.

DON'T SHOUT AT PEOPLE WHO ARE TRYING TO READ

Layout

D-13 · TIGHT CLEARSpace

Don't crowd the mark.

The mark needs 0.4× mark-height clearspace on all four sides. Crowding the mark weakens its recognition at small sizes.

D-14 · OFF-GRID

Don't lay out on a free grid.

DTPR lives on a 24px base unit. Margins, gutters, padding, and component dimensions snap to the grid. Free-floating pixels break the maker register.

x: 7px (off-grid)
D-15 · BUSY BACKGROUNDS

Don't put type over a busy texture.

The graph-paper substrate is 18% opacity at most. Dense textures behind text cause contrast failures. Either commit to the texture (no text) or commit to the text (no texture).

Hard to read
