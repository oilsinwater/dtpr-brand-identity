    Reference · Recipe A (Regulatory)

    Recipe A — archived visual system

    Recipe A was the original Industrial Spec Sheet direction: procurement-ready, ISO-compliant, regulator-friendly. As of v0.4 of these guidelines, Recipe A is no longer a separate recipe — it has been folded into Recipe B as an optional register for surfaces that need procurement or compliance framing without departing from the B lockup.

    MEMO OBSERVED

    Status: archived. Recipe A is referenced here for context only. Elements from Recipe A may inform specific Recipe B surfaces (procurement pages, compliance docs, signage specs), but they should not be imported wholesale into the Recipe B lockup. The Brand Identity Guidelines remain authoritative for Recipe B as the canonical direction.

    What Recipe A was

    Industrial Spec Sheet — a browser-based standards artifact that borrows the visual grammar of engineering documentation, compliance sheets, and public-technology specification records. Official without impersonating a government agency; exact without becoming hostile or bureaucratic.

    Primary audience: Pragmatic Deployer — city CIOs, public works directors, procurement officers, municipal procurement staff, and technology vendors.

    Recipe A type system (archived)

    Recipe A signature elements

    • 0px corner radii — no rounded corners anywhere. The Industrial Spec Sheet is sharp, not pillowy.
    • Hexagon grids and ISO 3864 framing — hexagon as the geometric primitive, warning-marker semantics aligned with ISO 3864.
    • Condensed sans display — Barlow Condensed ExtraBold or a similar condensed sans at display weight.
    • No motion — static, reliable, no entrance animations or scroll-triggered reveals.
    • Navy + light gray — DTPR navy #002684 on light gray surfaces #F0F2F5.
    • Safety colors — yellow, red, green reserved for warning/caution/certified semantics respectively.

    When to use A-flavored B

    Use sparingly. Most Recipe B surfaces should not need A flavor. The legitimate cases:

    • Procurement pages where deployers need ISO compliance framing.
    • Compliance and signage specification documents.
    • Discrepancy-reporting flows.
    • Pages where the audience is unambiguously the Pragmatic Deployer.

    When you reach for A flavor, ask first: does this page need procurement framing, or is it a Technical Evaluator surface that happens to mention procurement? The Technical Evaluator is the Recipe B primary audience. If the answer is "the latter," stay in pure B.
