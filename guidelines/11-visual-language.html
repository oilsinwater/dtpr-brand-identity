Developer Documentation & REST Schema

Developer and municipal documentation follows a rigorous technical scale. Public Sans delivers clear headings and prose, while Source Code Pro formats endpoints, method verbs, and schema properties on dark terminal slates.

Partner Co-Branding (Horizontal Lockup)

In co-branded and municipal partnership applications, the horizontal DTPR mark anchors the partnership hierarchy, separated from the partner mark by a 1px neutral rule on paper. The horizontal lockup is the correct choice here: partner marks are themselves horizontal, and matching the axis keeps both identities on a single optical baseline. Reserve the stacked mark for square and vertical formats.

Partner co-branding — Horizontal DTPR navy mark + 1px vertical divider + partner identity on paper substrate

Slide Deck Cover (Cornered Stacked Mark)

Slide deck covers set a centred Source Serif 4 italic title on deep Brand Navy (#1B2B80), with the stacked mark held small in the top right corner. The mark identifies the deck without competing with the title, and the title carries the cover on its own — no eyebrow label above it.

What makes the language work

The consolidated DTPR visual language succeeds by balancing technical precision with civic accessibility. Rather than adhering to a rigid or sterile developer template, it blends Recipe B’s "Maker" register (monospace telemetry, schema keys, 3D exploded urban layers) with dynamic aura fields, a split light/dark theme, and tactile warm paper surfaces (#F4F2EC). The split is structural, not decorative: paper sections are interrupted by solid navy bands and cards, and that alternation is what gives each page its rhythm.

By breaking the binary between cold technical documentation and warm public communication, the identity establishes DTPR as both an auditable open-source protocol and an inviting public trust standard.
