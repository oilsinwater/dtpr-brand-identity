15 · Co-Branding

Partner marks beside the DTPR mark.

How the DTPR mark sits next to a partner organization, a funder, or a host institution. Co-branding follows the same logic as the wordmark lockup: equal clearspace, equal visual weight, no hierarchy implied by ordering.

LOCKUP CLEARSPACE PARTNER MARKS

The default lockup (light surface)

Two marks, vertical divider, equal height, horizontal alignment. The DTPR mark goes on the left in most contexts; partner mark on the right.

Standard horizontal lockup · light surface

Inverted lockup (dark surface)

Same geometry. The DTPR mark uses its navy-on-navy inverted variant; partner mark gets the white-on-dark treatment.

Standard horizontal lockup · dark surface

The four lockup patterns

PATTERN 01

DTPR left, partner right

Default horizontal lockup. Use when the partner is the host (event, deployment, study) and DTPR is the standard they're implementing.

PATTERN 02

Partner left, DTPR right

When the partner is the host institution and DTPR is a guest (a panel, a partnership announcement). Reverses the lockup but keeps the same vertical divider.

PATTERN 03

Stacked (vertical)

For narrow surfaces — newsletter footers, certificate headers, certificate footers. Mark on top, partner below, divided by a horizontal hairline.

PATTERN 04

Three-party lockup

DTPR + host partner + funder. Same horizontal pattern, marks separated by 1px hairline dividers, each mark at equal cap height.

Clearspace and sizing

Rule Specification
Divider gap 1px vertical line, color var(--gray-300) (light) or rgba(255,255,255,0.2) (dark). Length = 0.4× mark height on either side of the smaller mark.
Clearspace around the lockup Equal to the cap-height of the smaller mark on all four sides. No body copy closer than this clearspace.
Cap-height equality Both marks rendered at the same cap-height (the top of the larger letterform). Partner marks must be optically sized to match — never auto-scaled by aspect ratio alone.
Minimum lockup width 240px on screen, 1.5in (38mm) in print. Below this, use the stacked pattern instead.
Three-party lockup Two dividers. Equal cap heights. Clearspace scales by the smallest mark's height.

What not to do

YES

Equal cap heights, single hairline divider, marks at the same baseline. The lockup reads as a partnership, not a hierarchy.

NO

One mark significantly larger than the other; no divider; words "presented by" or "in partnership with" layered between the marks. Hierarchy implies ownership — the lockup shouldn't.

YES

Partner mark in its canonical treatment (color or monochrome, as supplied). DTPR mark in its canonical navy. No restyling either mark to match.

NO

Recoloring the partner mark to navy so it "matches." Recolor the partner mark to fit a chartreuse accent. Either mark's brand guidelines forbid arbitrary recoloring.
