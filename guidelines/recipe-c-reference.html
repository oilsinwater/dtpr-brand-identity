    Reference · Recipe C (Commons)

    Recipe C — archived visual system

    Recipe C was the original Split Ground direction: bilateral dark/cream split, salmon-as-warmth accent, civic blue for actions only. As of v0.4 of these guidelines, Recipe C is no longer a separate recipe — it has been folded into Recipe B as an optional register for surfaces that need humanist warmth without departing from the B lockup.

    MEMO OBSERVED

    Status: archived. Recipe C is referenced here for context only. Elements from Recipe C may inform specific Recipe B surfaces (advocacy-aligned pages, humanist warmth sections, community-facing features), but they should not be imported wholesale into the Recipe B lockup. The Brand Identity Guidelines remain authoritative for Recipe B as the canonical direction.

    What Recipe C was

    The Split Ground — a bilateral page where the left half is dark ground and the right half is corpus cream. The split is the message: a public technology world that is, on one side, dark and weight-bearing, and on the other, warm and legible. Both halves are real, both are the page.

    Primary audience: Vigilant Advocate (community organizers, journalists, privacy researchers, digital rights campaigners) and Impacted Citizen (someone who encountered a DTPR-marked sensor in their neighbourhood).

    Recipe C signature elements

    • Bilateral dark/cream split — #0F1414 dark ground + #F0E7D6 corpus cream. The split is the page.
    • Salmon #E08373 — the human register. Hand-drawn motif highlights, emotional section dividers. Never used on actionable elements.
    • Civic blue #2A4B8C — the instruction register. Reserved exclusively for links, primary CTAs, focus rings.
    • 4–8px card radii — sharp enough to read as a real community document; never above 16px.
    • Organic motifs — corpus motifs (mycelial network, etc.) used at the dark/cream boundary as a connector, not as decoration.
    • Responsive motion only — gentle staggered fade-in on page load, hover lifts, prefers-reduced-motion respected.

    When to use C-flavored B

    Use sparingly. Most Recipe B surfaces should not need C flavor. The legitimate cases:

    • Surfaces that explain DTPR's impact on communities (citizen-facing sections of an otherwise-developer site).
    • Pages where the audience is the Vigilant Advocate or Impacted Citizen rather than the Technical Evaluator.
    • Documentation sections that benefit from humanist warmth (impact stories, deployment evidence with people).
    • A Recipe B section that needs to acknowledge the human stakes of public technology without becoming a separate Recipe C page.

    When you reach for C flavor, ask first: is this a Technical Evaluator surface that benefits from warmth, or is it a different recipe's audience dressed in B clothes? If the latter, the audience targeting is wrong — fix the audience, not the visual register.
