04 · Watermark

The watermark variant

A reduced-opacity version of the mark used as a background element on signage, slide decks, official letterhead, and overlay contexts where the mark is decorative rather than identifying. The watermark is never the primary brand signal — when in doubt, use the canonical mark, not the watermark.

PROPOSED

Construction

The watermark is the canonical mark rendered at reduced opacity. Three target contexts, three target opacities.

Opacity: 10%

Title slide background. The watermark sits behind a headline — present but never competing with the type.

Opacity: 15%

DTPR-deployed signage. The watermark marks a surface where DTPR is in use — decorative, not identifying.

Opacity: 20%

Hero overlay. On dark surfaces, the watermark reinforces the visual identity without drawing focus from the headline.

When to use the watermark

Recommended
  • Slide deck title slides, official letterhead, and presentation backgrounds.
  • Signage overlays where the mark is decorative (not identifying).
  • Print collateral covers where the mark sits behind a title.
  • Dark hero overlays where the mark reinforces the visual identity without competing with the headline.
Avoid
  • As the primary brand signal in a header or footer (use the canonical mark).
  • At opacities below 8% (becomes invisible) or above 25% (becomes a foreground mark).
  • Behind body copy where it reduces legibility.
  • In chartreuse, salmon, or any accent color. Watermark is monochrome.
