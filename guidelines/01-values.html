01 · Brand Identity Values

What the brand identity carries

Three personality words. One register descriptor. These are not aspirational — they are operational rules. Every visual decision is tested against them. If a choice cannot justify itself against this page, it does not belong in the identity.

The three personality words

Plain

The standard speaks to non-specialists. The city manager who is not a designer. The resident who is not a privacy lawyer. The council aide who is not a civic-tech person. Plain language is the first job of the brand identity.

Accountable

DTPR is the standard that says show your work. The brand identity has to show its work first. If the deployer is required to disclose X, the brand identity's own surfaces disclose X. Accountable is the operating principle, not a personality trait.

Plural

The standard is community-owned, not product-owned. DTPR does not have a customer. It has adopters, contributors, translators, advocates, residents, deployers, integrators — and it answers to all of them.

The register descriptor

The personality is what the brand identity is. The register is how the voice, typography, and visual language communicate.

Legible · Calm

The brand identity should be legible in the design sense (type, spacing, line-height, language all working together) and calm in the voice sense (no urgency, no scarcity, no countdown timers). The 35-second homepage bounce is a voice problem — the page reads as marketing prose. The fix is a calmer register.

What the brand identity is not

The negatives do more work than the positives. Six rules.

Not a product

No pricing. No "Get a demo." No marketing prose. DTPR is a standard you adopt, not a tool you buy.

Not a government agency

No seal-and-eagle. No "Pursuant to Section…." The standard is for public use, not for governing, even though the adopters are governments.

Not an AI vendor

The AI track uses the same visual system as the physical-sensor track. Same hexagons, same taxonomy, same chartreuse signal. Different surface, same cohesive brand identity.

Not the legacy Corporate Memphis aesthetic

Geometric blob people, lavender gradients, "playful" geometry — deprecated by Phase 2 consensus. The brand identity guidelines name this as retired.

Not a policy site

Long body copy in small serif, footer-of-100-links, "this policy was last updated…". DTPR is a standard you adopt, not a policy you publish. The privacy policy lives at dtpr.io/privacy if it needs to be referenced; the rest of the surface is the standard, not a policy.

Not a vendor lock-in

The CC BY 4.0 story has to be visible in the visual register. A graph-paper field reads as specification, which reads as open.

The four audiences

DTPR's visual identity has to work across four distinct audiences without compromising any of them.

Pragmatic Deployers

CIOs, city leaders, procurement officers. They hold the budget. They need procurement-ready language and a regulator-friendly, low-risk instrument.

Vigilant Advocates

Civic tech, non-profits, journalists, privacy researchers. They push the agenda. They need decentralized trust systems, clear data hierarchies, and collaborative feedback loops.

Impacted Citizens

People who encounter a DTPR-marked sensor. They need maximum simplicity, plain language, and neutral static wayfinding labels.

Technical Evaluators

Developers, integrators, open-source contributors. They need modular API specifications, monospaced code blocks, and copy-pasteable assets.
