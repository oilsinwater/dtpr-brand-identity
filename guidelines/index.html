    Digital Trust for Places & Routines. How the DTPR identity is constructed, used, and protected — values, logo, color, type, photography, and visual language in one place.

    What this is. A prescriptive reference for how the DTPR identity is built and used — the values the brand carries, the marks it presents, the colors and fonts it draws from, and the visual language it produces when those parts are combined. What it is not. An audit of what exists in the wild (see the Brand Audit). This document is the rules; the audit is the observations.

    Table of contents

    Eleven pages, ordered from intent (values) through identity elements (logo, stack, watermark, icon, dimensions) to execution rules (color, fonts, font protocol) to applied examples (photography, visual language).

    01 Brand Values Plain. Accountable. Calm. Legible. The personality words and register descriptor that govern every identity decision. 02 Logo The DTPR mark, the canonical wordmark lockup, and the navy-solid default. Three lockup variants: blue solid, black solid, outline. 03 Logo (Stacked) The stacked mark + wordmark lockup for square containers, vertical layouts, and small-format displays. 04 Watermark The reduced-opacity variant for backgrounds, signage overlays, and slide decks. Visibility rules. 05 Icon The icon (DTPR mark alone, no wordmark) in five treatments: brand colors, inverted, black & white, monochrome, inverse monochrome. 06 Dimensions & Clear Space Minimum sizes for digital and print. Clearspace rule (0.4× mark height). Forbidden modifications. 07 Color Guide The Recipe B palette — navy, chartreuse (reserved), warm paper, terminal dark, semantic status. Contrast ratios. Chartreuse reservation rule. 08 Fonts Public Sans (display + body + UI), Source Serif 4 Italic (editorial moment, hero only), Source Code Pro (code, taxonomy codes only). 09 Font Protocol Do's and don'ts. Where Source Serif 4 Italic may appear (once per page, hero). Where Source Code Pro may appear (code, taxonomy only). Mono is not for UI. 10 Photography Handwritten notes, study notebooks, planning sketches. The photographic register. Examples that work; examples to avoid. 11 Visual Language Logo + motifs + colors + fonts together. Applied examples of the identity in action. R1 Recipe A Reference (archived) The Industrial Spec Sheet direction — procurement-ready, ISO-compliant. Available as an optional A-flavored register within Recipe B. R2 Recipe C Reference (archived) The Split Ground direction — bilateral dark/cream, salmon-as-warmth, civic blue for actions. Available as an optional C-flavored register within Recipe B.

    How to read this document

    Read straight through if you are new to DTPR. If you are promoting the identity in a specific surface, jump to the relevant page. The font protocol (09) is the page most often violated — treat it as the rule book.

    This document is the second section of the DTPR brand book. The other section is the Brand Audit (what exists in the wild). Together, the two sections form a complete narrative: observation → rules.
