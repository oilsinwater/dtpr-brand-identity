Identity Guidelines

Digital Trust for Places & Routines. How the DTPR identity is constructed, used, and protected — values, logo, color, type, photography, and visual language in one place.

What this is. A prescriptive reference for how the DTPR identity is built and used — the values the brand identity carries, the marks it presents, the colors and fonts it draws from, and the visual language it produces when those parts are combined. What it is not. An audit of what exists in the wild (see the Brand Audit). This document is the rules; the audit is the observations.

Table of contents

Twenty-two pages, ordered from intent (values) through identity elements (logo, stack, watermark, icon, dimensions) to execution rules (color, fonts, font protocol), applied examples (photography, visual language), the production prototype (12), then the rules of use (13–15) and the application library (16–22).

01 Brand Values Plain. Accountable. Calm. Legible. The personality words and register descriptor that govern every identity decision. 02 Logo The DTPR mark, the canonical wordmark lockup, and the navy-solid default. Three lockup variants: blue solid, black solid, outline. 03 Logo (Stacked) The stacked mark + wordmark lockup for square containers, vertical layouts, and small-format displays. 04 Watermark The reduced-opacity variant for backgrounds, signage overlays, and slide decks. Visibility rules. 05 Icon The icon (DTPR mark alone, no wordmark) in five treatments: brand colors, inverted, black & white, monochrome, inverse monochrome. 06 Dimensions & Clear Space Minimum sizes for digital and print. Clearspace rule (0.4× mark height). Forbidden modifications. 07 Color Guide The Recipe B palette — navy, chartreuse (reserved), warm paper, terminal dark, semantic status. Contrast ratios. Chartreuse reservation rule. 08 Fonts Public Sans (display + body + UI), Source Serif 4 Italic (editorial moment, hero only), Source Code Pro (code, taxonomy codes only). 09 Font Protocol Do's and don'ts. Where Source Serif 4 Italic may appear (once per page, hero). Where Source Code Pro may appear (code, taxonomy only). Mono is not for UI. 10 Photography Handwritten notes, study notebooks, planning sketches. The photographic register. Examples that work; examples to avoid. 11 Visual Language Logo + motifs + colors + fonts together. Applied examples of the identity in action. 12 Web Application (V3) Production implementation of the visual identity system — flat triangulated mesh map, light-mode journey phone, pare-down edition. Embeds the live V3 prototype. 13 Misuse & Don'ts Explicit examples of what the visual identity is not — anti-patterns and off-spec misuse. Don't stretch the mark, don't put chartreuse on text, don't compress letter spacing. The rules through their violations. 14 Voice & Tone How DTPR writes — voice and tone standards. Plain. Accountable. Calm. Legible. applied to headlines, body copy, CTAs, captions, errors, and deployment descriptions. 15 Co-Branding Partner and funder marks beside the DTPR mark. Equal cap heights, hairline divider, four lockup patterns. 16 Print Collateral One-pagers, brochures, posters, reports. Token-driven print applications of the brand. 17 Stationery Business cards, letterhead, envelopes, email signatures. The surfaces an org actually sends. 18 Environmental & Signage Booth banners, step-and-repeat backdrops, civic signage plaques. The brand at scale. 19 Merch & Swag Tees, totes, stickers, pins, patches. Apparel and accessories that steward the brand. 20 Motion Lower thirds, intro/outro cards, loading states. Animation that informs, not performs. 21 Email Templates Newsletter, event invitation, donation ask. Plain. Accountable. Calm. Legible. in the inbox. 22 App UI App icon, favicon, splash, empty state, error state, dark variant. The surfaces the user actually touches.

How to read this document

Read straight through if you are new to DTPR. If you are promoting the identity in a specific surface, jump to the relevant page. The font protocol (09) is the page most often violated — treat it as the rule book.

This document is the second section of the DTPR brand book. The other section is the Brand Audit (what exists in the wild). Together, the two sections form a complete narrative: observation → rules.
