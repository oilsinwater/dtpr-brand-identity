14 · Voice & Tone

How DTPR communicates in copy and voice.

DTPR's visual register is calm and legible; the writing matches. Short sentences, plain words, no marketing inflection. The voice is the same across surfaces — the tone adapts to context.

WRITING GUIDE EDITORIAL RECIPE B

The four values, applied to writing

From 01 Values. The personality words govern copy decisions the way they govern color decisions.

Plain
Short sentences. Common words. The reader should never need a dictionary.
Accountable
Names the actor. Says who did it, who decides, who pays. No passive voice hiding the steward.
Calm
No exclamation marks. No marketing italics. States the case and stops.
Legible
Plain language. Active voice. Reads aloud without stumbling.

What to write, and how

HEADLINES

One claim, written like a person.

Headlines state what DTPR is, not what it does for you. Subject first; benefit second; never "Welcome to..."

YES

A standard for accountable technology.

NO

Welcome to the future of public trust!

BODY COPY

Read aloud.

Long sentences that you can't read aloud without gasping are wrong. Aim for 12–18 words per sentence in body copy.

YES

Sensors arrive with procurement notices, but with nothing a person walking past can actually read.

NO

In today's rapidly evolving civic technology landscape, where intelligent infrastructure increasingly shapes the lived experience of urban spaces, the imperative for transparency has never been more paramount.

CTAs

Verb-first, no exclamation.

CTAs say what happens next. "Explore", "View", "Subscribe", "Get the standard". One or two words. No "Click here!", no "Sign up now >>".

YES

Explore the Standard → · Subscribe · View taxonomy

NO

CLICK HERE TO LEARN MORE!!! · Sign up now >>

CAPTIONS & METADATA

Lowercase, mono, factual.

Captions and schema keys are monospace, lowercase, terminal in register. They're for people who already know what they're looking at.

YES

location: civic plaza, pole #104

NO

Welcome to our Beautiful Civic Plaza where Amazing Pole #104 is Located!

ERROR MESSAGES

State the problem, give the next step.

Errors should not apologize, blame the user, or hedge. State what happened in one sentence and what to do next.

YES

Could not reach the taxonomy endpoint. Retrying in 5s. Refresh to try sooner.

NO

Oops! Something went wrong. We're sorry for the inconvenience. Please try again later!

DEPLOYMENT DESCRIPTIONS

Cite the steward and the scope.

When describing a deployment, name who runs it and what it covers. Avoid adjectives that could become false claims.

YES

Standardized digital rights signage deployed by the City of Long Beach across civic plazas and municipal facilities.

NO

A pioneering world-class deployment showcasing best-in-class transparency innovation!

The hard rules

Rule Why
No exclamation marks. The calm register requires them. Save the energy for the substance.
No "we're excited to..." / "we're proud to...". Tell the reader what's useful. They're not here for the team's feelings.
No "leverage", "unlock", "powerful", "robust", "seamless". These are filler. Name the specific thing the standard does instead.
No second-person marketing ("you can..."). State the standard's capabilities factually. The reader infers what they can do.
Cite the steward of every deployment. Accountability is core to the brand identity. Don't say "a city" — say which city, which department.
Numbers are facts. Don't round to look better. "17 cities" beats "20+ communities". Specificity is the trust signal.
One italic sentence per page. The italic editorial moment is fundamental. Don't dilute it.
